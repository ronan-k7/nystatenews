Trump to Pardon Ex-Puerto Rico Governor Wanda Vázquez Garced in Campaign Finance Case, Official Says

Former U.S. President Donald Trump is expected to pardon former Puerto Rico Governor Wanda Vázquez Garced in a campaign finance case, according to a senior official. The decision could end a high-profile federal prosecution that has drawn attention across the United States and Puerto Rico.

Published: 12th March 2026

Former U.S. President Donald Trump is expected to issue a presidential pardon to former Puerto Rico Governor Wanda Vázquez Garced in connection with a federal campaign finance case, according to a senior official familiar with the decision. The anticipated pardon would effectively end one of the most closely watched political corruption cases involving a top official from the U.S.

territory and has already sparked intense debate about presidential clemency, political accountability, and federal oversight in Puerto Rico. The case against Vázquez Garced has been under scrutiny for several years and has drawn widespread attention both on the island and in Washington. Federal prosecutors accused the former governor of participating in a scheme involving illegal campaign contributions and political favors. According to court filings, investigators alleged that individuals seeking favorable treatment from the Puerto Rican government arranged financial support for her political campaign through intermediaries and consulting arrangements designed to conceal the origin of the funds. Vázquez Garced, who served as governor of Puerto Rico from 2019 to 2021, has consistently denied the allegations.

Her legal team argued throughout the proceedings that the accusations were based on weak evidence and misinterpretations of routine political activities. Defense attorneys maintained that the prosecution relied heavily on testimony from cooperating witnesses who were themselves facing legal consequences and had incentives to implicate others. The legal battle reached a critical moment when a federal jury was unable to reach a unanimous verdict during a previous trial, resulting in a mistrial. Prosecutors later indicated they were considering whether to retry the case, leaving Vázquez facing continued legal uncertainty.

Supporters of the former governor argued that the mistrial demonstrated reasonable doubt and that the government should not pursue another attempt at conviction. If the pardon is formally granted, it would immediately terminate any remaining federal legal exposure related to the charges. Under the U.S. Constitution, the president has the authority to grant pardons for federal crimes, a power that has historically been used to show mercy, correct perceived injustices, or resolve politically sensitive cases.

A presidential pardon does not necessarily determine guilt or innocence, but it removes the legal penalties associated with the offense and prevents further prosecution for those specific charges. Trump's expected decision reflects his broader views on the criminal justice system and federal prosecutions. Throughout his political career, he has repeatedly criticized federal investigators and prosecutors, claiming that some cases are driven by political motivations rather than clear evidence of wrongdoing. Supporters of the former president argue that pardons can serve as an important check against government overreach and lengthy legal battles that may not ultimately result in convictions. The potential pardon has already generated strong reactions across Puerto Rico, where public trust in political institutions has been deeply affected by years of corruption scandals and economic challenges.

For many residents, federal prosecutions have played a key role in holding public officials accountable. Several mayors, legislators, and government administrators on the island have faced corruption charges over the past decade, leading to calls for greater transparency and stricter ethics rules. Critics of the expected pardon argue that it could undermine those efforts. Anti-corruption advocates say that granting clemency in a high-profile political case risks sending a message that powerful figures can avoid consequences through political connections.

Some civic organizations have warned that such a decision could weaken public confidence in the rule of law and discourage future investigations into misconduct. Political leaders in Puerto Rico have also responded with mixed reactions. Some officials and commentators believe the pardon would be a justified step given the mistrial and the uncertainty surrounding the case. They argue that prolonging the legal process would only deepen political divisions and distract from more urgent issues facing the island, including economic recovery, infrastructure development, and hurricane preparedness. Others, however, see the situation very differently.

Opponents say that the case should be allowed to run its course through the courts without political intervention. They contend that presidential clemency in cases involving elected officials must be used cautiously, particularly when allegations involve campaign finance violations or abuse of public power. Vázquez Garced's path to the governorship was itself shaped by political turmoil. She assumed office in August 2019 following the resignation of Governor Ricardo Rosselló, who stepped down after massive protests erupted across Puerto Rico.

Demonstrators demanded his resignation after the release of leaked messages that many residents considered offensive and inappropriate for a public official. At the time, Vázquez was serving as secretary of justice and initially expressed reluctance to assume the governorship. However, after a constitutional succession process and a series of legal challenges, she ultimately took office and pledged to restore stability and public trust. Her administration faced significant challenges, including economic difficulties, the aftermath of devastating hurricanes, and the COVID-19 pandemic. During her tenure, Vázquez attempted to position herself as a reform-minded leader focused on improving government transparency and strengthening public institutions.

Nevertheless, political tensions remained high, and her administration often faced criticism from opposition parties and activist groups demanding deeper structural reforms. The federal investigation that eventually led to charges against her became a major turning point in her political career. Prosecutors alleged that campaign donors sought government contracts and regulatory advantages in exchange for financial support. The accusations triggered a lengthy legal process that dominated headlines and overshadowed other political developments in Puerto Rico. Despite the controversy, Vázquez has continued to maintain her innocence.

In public statements, she has said that the case placed a heavy emotional burden on her family and damaged her reputation. She has argued that the mistrial confirmed the weaknesses in the prosecution's arguments and that the legal proceedings should have ended at that point. If the pardon proceeds as expected, it will likely reignite debates about the use of presidential clemency in politically sensitive cases. Throughout U.S.

history, presidents have occasionally granted pardons to high-profile political figures, sometimes drawing praise for acts of reconciliation and sometimes criticism for appearing to shield allies from accountability. Legal scholars note that the presidential pardon power is intentionally broad and largely immune from judicial review. The framers of the Constitution believed the authority could serve as a safeguard against unjust prosecutions or overly harsh punishments. At the same time, critics argue that the power can be controversial when applied in cases involving political allies or individuals connected to powerful networks. In the context of Puerto Rico, the situation also raises questions about the island's political relationship with the federal government.

As a U.S. territory, Puerto Rico operates under federal jurisdiction but does not have full voting representation in Congress. Some commentators say that high-profile legal decisions involving the island often highlight the complexities of that arrangement. Public opinion on the expected pardon remains deeply divided.

Supporters of Vázquez believe the move would finally close a painful chapter in Puerto Rican politics and allow the former governor to rebuild her personal and professional life. They argue that years of legal uncertainty and negative publicity have already taken a significant toll. Critics, however, insist that the broader fight against corruption must remain a priority. They say that regardless of the outcome of any single case, systemic reforms are necessary to strengthen campaign finance transparency, enforce ethical standards, and rebuild public confidence in government institutions. For Trump, the decision would represent another example of his willingness to use executive authority to challenge federal prosecutions he considers questionable.

Throughout his political career, he has emphasized the importance of confronting what he views as politically motivated investigations. His supporters see this stance as a defense of fairness in the justice system, while opponents argue that it risks politicizing legal processes. As of now, officials have indicated that the pardon could be announced formally in the near future, though the exact timing remains unclear. If confirmed, the move would mark a significant development in a case that has captured public attention for years. Regardless of how the political debate unfolds, the situation underscores the continuing tension between law, politics, and public trust.

In Puerto Rico, where questions of governance and accountability remain central issues, the outcome of the Vázquez case is likely to influence discussions about political reform and federal oversight for years to come..
